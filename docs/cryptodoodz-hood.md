# The Block: playable neighborhood

Route: `/cryptodoodz/world/`. Original CryptoDoodz models are loaded on demand from the existing collection. The shared Walk, Run, Jump and Shoot animations drive the avatar. Each new local profile receives a Glock, compact car, $500 game cash and ammunition.

Controls: WASD move, Shift sprint (stamina), mouse aim, click shoot, R reload, Space jump, E business interaction, F enter/exit a nearby car, Esc release pointer lock. Right-drag provides a camera fallback where pointer lock is unavailable.

Businesses: police station delivery jobs ($180), corner store snacks/ammo, weapon store with three guns, vehicle dealership with three cars, garage repairs. Target range awards $25 per destroyed target; targets respawn. Shooting outside the range raises police heat, triggering a simple pursuing patrol and a $75 citation. Cars have arcade acceleration/steering, collisions and condition-dependent top speed.

Current scope is a solo desktop browser sandbox with localStorage progress. All five businesses and 16 apartment buildings have walkable, furnished ground floors with open entrances. Upper apartment floors are exterior facades; the distant skyline is scenery outside the playable boundary. Business interactions and delivery completion are at interior service counters. NPC pedestrians are ambient; combat is against range targets. There is no shared server, PvP, NFT ownership enforcement or real-money economy. Save state is intentionally client-controlled. Do not use it as an authoritative economy for multiplayer or on-chain rewards.

World geometry/materials are procedural and reused. The avatar and its six clips are existing collection assets. The Glock is a stylized game prop; all weapon behavior is game-only.

The neighborhood now has warm afternoon lighting with filmic tone mapping, brick accents, striped awnings, rooftop equipment, produce displays, crosswalks, benches, planters and a distant skyline. Static details use instanced meshes grouped by material. Eight distinct collection models (38, 981, 64, 185, 296, 4, 10 and 67) walk sidewalk routes and pause using their original Idle/Walk clips. NPC loading is sequential and independent of the player; distant NPC animations are throttled and hidden beyond 62 units. They are ambient residents, not combat targets.

NPC validation: `node --test tests/hood-npcs.test.mjs` imports all eight actual GLBs, checks Idle/Walk clips and samples their routes against building collision footprints.

On foot, the camera sits 1.05 units over the right shoulder and 4.8 units back, keeping the character left of the center reticle. Camera collision includes the lateral offset and preserves the aim direction when pushed inward. Shots resolve the reticle target, then check the muzzle path for nearby cover. Driving retains its centered chase view. The top navbar is removed; Esc opens the menu even without pointer lock, and the collection link lives inside that menu. Camera projection tests cover four headings and the driving view.

Tests: `node --test tests/hood.test.mjs` covers spawn inventory, purchases/overdraft prevention, save recovery and collision boundaries. Svelte/TypeScript diagnostics and the production build validate integration. Live gameplay/browser automation has not been performed in this task.

Walkable interiors use wall segments around 3.2-unit door openings, overhead lintels and ceilings, floor surfaces, and furniture collision footprints. Corner Mart has grocery aisles and fridges; police has reception, desks and lockers; Block Arms has display cases; the garage has a service bay; the dealership has a showroom car and lounge. Homes have living, kitchen and sleeping areas. Nearby interiors are shown within 38 units; two lights follow the occupied room. On foot the indoor shoulder camera uses a 2.7-unit distance and .65-unit offset. Doorways are open continuously; no teleport or separate map is required. Tests build the real furniture collision layout and search entry/exit/counter paths for every building.

Expanded city: playable bounds are now +/-158 units (previously +/-68), with a 336-unit map, five north/south and five east/west streets, 25 junctions and an outer driving loop. Dealership moved to (46,-23), the road-blocking apartment moved to (-49,-23). Shared layout data defines all road axes and residential lots. All 23 buildings retain enterable ground floors. Southside Park includes a basketball court. Road-clearance tests check every building setback and sweep both lanes with a car footprint against actual furniture/wall colliders. The minimap and district names follow the expanded map; skyline moved beyond its boundary and sunlight follows the player.

Doodz Plaza at (110,-110) replaces one Northside house with an enterable mall: three functioning shop counters, lounge and food court. Northside Parking at (-110,-110) replaces another house with a single-level covered garage, 12-unit vehicle entrance, marked stalls and a central aisle. Both appear on the minimap. Mall counters are delivery destinations; deliveries now match the exact counter rather than business type. Apartment name boards occupy a reserved band at y=4.7; upper windows and trims begin above it. Distant pillar placeholders were replaced with broader city blocks, stepped roofs and window rows. Regression tests verify facade separation and navigable landmark paths.
